# Hero line: the owner's brief

Everything the owner has asked for, in their own terms, so it survives a
context compaction and so any agent picking this up inherits the intent rather
than guessing it from the code.

Companion docs: [`hero-line-frontiers.md`](hero-line-frontiers.md) (the rubric)
and [`hero-line-journal.md`](hero-line-journal.md) (the run log).

Surface: `/lab/line`. Renderer: `app/lib/hero-line/renderer.ts`.

---

## The sentence the picture has to say

Left to right, with no caption:

> Raw session files fly in and **dissolve** at a boundary → they **coalesce**
> into a finished film strip → the strip runs through the frame carrying the
> titles that were applied to it → it reaches a second boundary and
> **disintegrates** → it leaves as a transmission arriving at the channels.

## Standing directives

### The thresholds (both ends)

- **"The portals are just grey boxes. Totally unacceptable."** They are not
  machines, bodies, plates or chips.
- **"Like the portal is just a thin ring of energy. Pure black in the middle.
  As things pass through, they transform."**
- **"More like these thresholds that are actually just like a thin swirl of
  smoke almost."**
- Left: files "slightly begin to disintegrate and quickly materialize into the
  film to the right of the boundary."
- Right: "you see the film disintegrate as it enters and turn into a
  transmission line."
- **The whole transition must fit in under ~40px of horizontal space.**

### Particles

- **"The materialization and dematerialization layers are pretty horrible."**
- **"Way too much particle soup."** "It's a huge cloud." It must not sprawl.
- It should read as *passing through a boundary and quickly coalescing*, not as
  a confetti field beside a film.

### The film strip

- **"It looks like a creeping caterpillar. It doesn't look 3D at all."**
- **"A film strip isn't elastic on any axis except the one you can fold it on.
  I can't take a film strip and make one edge narrower or one edge wider. It's
  not rubbery like that."** ← the governing physical constraint.
- The classic look, from the reference images the owner sent: solid black
  rails, bright perforations, hard black rules between frames.
- **"If we're using the wrong tool for the job, maybe we should evaluate that
  too."** The 2D-canvas choice is not sacred.

### Composition

- **The film strip is the centre of the composition.** Everything else is
  service.
- The transmission "doesn't need to take up so much horizontal space."
- The composition was called out as **imbalanced** when the mass sat left of
  centre with a dead band beneath.

### Titles

- Not floating pills on tether lines. **"I was literally picturing actual
  captions flying in and attaching to the show."**

### Destinations

- YouTube, Podcast, LinkedIn. **Keep it conceptual** — "YouTube also absorbs
  shorts," so the picture must not assert a 16:9-here / 9:16-there split.
- The output is a **transmission**, not film travelling to a website.

### The cut

- Removed, deliberately. **"It's just another transform."** Do not reintroduce
  hatching, offcuts, or a cutting-room floor.

### Page chrome

- Keep the top nav, the headline, and the CTA. An earlier instruction to strip
  them was withdrawn.

## How the work is to be done

- **"Scaling inference here, doing adversarial review, drawing out the best
  from each other."** `grok` and `agy` are to be used heavily and continuously,
  not once per session — see `docs/build/visual-review-appendages.md` in the
  product repo.
- **Ask them what questions we should be asking**, not only for critiques.
- **Round-robin protocol.** Rotate through concept, film geometry, particles,
  composition and layout, dimensionality, material and value, frontier
  technique, motion, type and hierarchy. Revisit anything; a deep pass on one
  subject does not close it.
- **A round can take an hour.** Depth over count. Diagnose properly — crops,
  contact sheets, telemetry — before changing anything.
- **Keep the journal.**

## The standard

> "Communicate this more clearly, more calmly, more beautifully."
>
> "Pushes the frontiers of web visualization technology."

Calm is load-bearing. When something is noisy, the answer is usually to
subtract rather than to restyle.
