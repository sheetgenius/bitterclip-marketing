## You recorded ninety minutes. Now what.

It is Tuesday. There is a ninety-minute session — a podcast, a client call, a workshop — either sitting on your drive or recorded straight into the browser, and by Friday it has to be one clean episode and four or five short clips, captioned, on brand, sent. Then the same thing happens next week.

CapCut can get you there. You will do it on a timeline: scrub, split, delete, scrub back, then find the same tangent a second time because the first cut clipped a syllable. Desktop transcript editing helps — right-click a clip, generate the transcript, edit the text, the video follows — but it is one tool tucked inside a consumer editor, not the way the app thinks.

BitterClip starts from the words. However the session got there, it arrives as prose you can read. Select the tangent, hit delete, and the video is genuinely cut — on the word, not near it, because the edit is worked out against the actual audio. No clipped syllables. No half-swallowed first word. And in October, when someone asks where a line came from, one click puts you back at that exact spot in the full recording.

## You can record it here too

The session does not have to arrive from somewhere else. Every project has a browser recorder: "Record in browser" on the Sources tab, or "Record here" in the editor's insert sheet, where the finished take drops into the edit at the playhead. Prop your phone up, or sit at a laptop, and it captures camera and mic — or your screen, in desktop Chrome — up to 1080p. It uploads in short chunks as you record, so nothing large is stranded on your device, the page shows you how much is already safely uploaded, and transcription starts the moment you press stop.

Know what it is not. One person, one device, signed in: no guest recording links, no separate track per participant, no 4K, no local backup copy if the network goes bad. It is not a remote interview studio. If your guest is somewhere else, record them in a tool built for that and bring the files in — that path is still completely normal, and for most people it is still the common one. Recording also needs billing on the account when transcription is switched on.

## Say what you want changed

The other difference is how you give the instruction. In CapCut you click. In BitterClip you can click too, or you can just ask — in ChatGPT, in Claude, or in the editor's own chat panel: cut the tangent around 14:20 and tighten the intro. What comes back is an ordinary edit. Open it, drag it, change your mind, undo it, all the way back. Anything you can click, you can ask for, and nothing your assistant does lands somewhere you cannot reach by hand.

Nothing scores your moments, either. CapCut's shorts tool watches for changes in visuals, audio, and context and decides what counts as a highlight. BitterClip has no such thing, on purpose. You point at the moment, or you describe what you are after and search everything you have recorded by what was said, who said it, or what was on screen.

## Keep CapCut for what it is genuinely great at

CapCut is a good editor and it is free. For short video made on a phone — trending templates, effects, sound, one tap to TikTok — it is the default for real reasons, and none of the above is an argument against it. If that is the work, stay where you are.

The switch is worth considering when the shape of the week changes: you are not making one short any more, you are finishing a recording, and then another recording.
